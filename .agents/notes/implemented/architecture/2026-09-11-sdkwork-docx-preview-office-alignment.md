# Agent Note: The Word preview splits flowing blocks, resolves table styles, and calibrates line metrics like Office

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-11-sdkwork-docx-preview-office-alignment.zh.md)

## Problem

The first renderer paginated by whole block: a paragraph that crossed a page boundary moved to the next page intact, so a page ended two lines into its height and a long paragraph could be clipped outright. Against a fixture battery checked against real Word output (each sample converted to PDF by Microsoft Word itself), two regression passes found twenty-plus gaps: `w:spacing/@line` had no effect because every run painted a hardcoded 1.25 line height that overrode the paragraph's; CSS margin collapsing took the larger of adjacent paragraph spacings where Word sums them; an empty paragraph was one default line tall instead of its paragraph mark's; space-before painted at the top of a page where Word suppresses it; table conditional formatting (`w:tblStylePr`) never resolved, so a styled table drew naked; a vertically merged continuation was matched by cell ordinal, landing on the wrong column whenever a horizontal span shifted the row; a `v:shape` without a drawable image silently vanished; `titlePg` and even/odd headers fell back to the default part where Word draws nothing; a story taller than its margin overlapped the body; `w:pgNumType/@start` numbering restarted nothing; a continuous section break forced a page break; sections without header parts lost the previous section's stories entirely; external hyperlinks answered any scheme including `javascript:`; every rail thumbnail mounted a full page tree; and the arrow keys flipped pages instead of scrolling.

## Decision

Two passes, both inside the existing parse → model → CSS projection, with no new dependency:

- **Flowing blocks split at real edges.** Pagination consumes measured content heights and per-block flow margins and delegates division to a splitter backed by live DOM maps: a paragraph divides at a line-box edge (leaving at least two lines on each side, Word's widow-and-orphan default; `w:keepLines` moves whole) and a table divides between rows, repeating its `w:tblHeader` rows on the continuation and refusing a cut that a vertical span reaches across. The measurement container lives exactly while a round runs, so division reads real geometry and the container then unmounts.
- **Line metrics follow Word.** A multiple `w:spacing/@line` counts the typeface's natural lines, so the body probes each font's natural line height once (cached per font-family) and turns multiples into absolute heights; exact and at-least rules stay pixel values. Flow margins sum the previous paragraph's space-after into the current block's top, the first block of a page drops its spacing, and the paragraph mark's typeface sizes an empty paragraph.
- **Tables resolve their declared style.** `w:tblStylePr` conditional bands (first/last row and column, horizontal and vertical banding) resolve through the style's `basedOn` chain under the table's `w:tblLook`: band shading and borders decorate the cells their area covers, and band run properties join the cascade under the paragraph style of the blocks those cells hold — cell blocks are read after grid placement, so the underlay lands exactly where the band does. A vertically merged continuation is matched by grid column; a continuation with no restart above draws empty, dropping its content the way Word does. Per-cell `w:tcMar`, `w:tblInd`, exact (clipping) and at-least row heights, and a percentage `w:tblW` that stretches proportionally all follow the file.
- **Sections behave like Word's.** A section without header or footer parts inherits the previous section's stories; `titlePg` and even/odd selection draw nothing when their part is absent instead of falling back; a story taller than its margin pushes the body area down (ResizeObserver-measured, so font settling re-runs it); `w:pgNumType/@start` seeds the numbering the reader sees in `PAGE` fields and the section's continuation carries it forward; a continuous break shares its page unless it declares stories of its own.
- **Links are filtered and internal ones navigate.** An external relationship answers only `http`, `https`, and `mailto` — anything else renders as styled text; an `w:anchor` link scrolls the stage to the paragraph holding the matching `w:bookmarkStart` (a `data-` attribute, so a bookmark named like a DOM global cannot clobber anything), and a `w:pict` without a drawable image names the object instead of vanishing.
- **The rail scales and the keys behave.** Thumbnails paint only around the rail's viewport through one IntersectionObserver, the rail scrolls to the selection, `PageCanvas` is memoized and `<img>` loads lazily, the rail collapses below a 520 px container query, the stage gains a focus ring, arrows scroll to the page edge before stepping (with `Home`/`End`), and Ctrl+wheel zooms through a non-passive native listener because React registers wheel handlers passively.

The Word body resets view state only when a value changes (an empty-pages reset with a fresh array identity would loop a rerun that receives unstable tab props), and the page's header growth, ratio probes, and settle round re-run after `document.fonts.ready` so late-loading typefaces re-calibrate before heights are read.

**Round three widened the sweep to what a clean fixture battery could not reach.** A third pass against fresh samples (right-to-left text, a header taller than its margin, portrait/landscape/portrait sections, a 26-page document) fixed the remaining structural gaps: a document's default character style re-applied the document defaults above the paragraph cascade, stomping every paragraph-level value (a Title rendered at body size); numbering counters now live per abstract definition, so instances sharing one continue the count like Word while an instance's `startOverride` still restarts its own level; tables without `w:tblLayout w:type="fixed"` use the browser's content-driven layout (an embedded table stretches its column; an auto-width table hugs its text); adjacent paragraphs with identical borders draw one shared box instead of a rule per line; the pagination loop lets a divided tail compete on its fresh page, so a table spanning several pages keeps flowing instead of clipping its remainder; and a right-to-left paragraph swaps the `left`/`right` justification edges the way Word reads them.

**A fourth pass closed the remaining content-loss and navigation gaps.** Structured document tags (`w:sdt`) unwrap at both block and inline level, so template content controls and the TOCs parked inside them read as if bare (the body walk, cell reader, and inline reader all recurse through `w:sdtContent`, and a section break inside one still ends a section). Custom tab stops (`w:tabs`) lay the following text out against their declared left, center, and right stops as flex segments — reproducing the left/center/right footer and the TOC's right-edge page number; leader lines remain undrawn. The toolbar's page counter became an editable page box (type a number, press Enter), clamped to the paginated range.

**A fifth pass covered reference marks, accessible names, and reading zoom.** A footnote or endnote reference renders its superscript sequence mark in the body (the note text itself stays a documented limitation); a picture's `wp:docPr/@descr` becomes the image's accessible name; and the fit toggle gained a fit-width mode that fills the pane's width, so wide tables and landscape pages stay readable in a narrow tab.

**A fifth pass covered East Asian indents, right-to-left tables, and page-flip ergonomics.** Word writes character-unit indents (`w:ind w:firstLineChars`, `leftChars`, `hangingChars`, `rightChars`) for East Asian documents; the paragraph resolves them against its mark's size, overriding the twips values. `w:bidiVisual` reverses a table's column order with mirrored horizontal borders, so Arabic and Hebrew tables read right to left. Flipping pages returns the stage scroll to the top of the new page.

## Alternatives considered

**A real layout engine.** Line-level pagination without re-flow keeps the model → CSS projection and the browser as the line breaker; a full engine (or OnlyOffice-class rewriting) was ruled out because it would abandon the pure model the keyless tests assert against. The cost is documented: a table row taller than a page clips rather than splitting mid-row, and a vertical span across a cut moves the table whole.

**Split a tall row's cells.** Word continues a row's text across pages; that needs per-cell column measurement and re-composition, so the split refuses the cut and moves the table instead — corruption was worse than a coarse break.

**Parse in a worker.** `DOMParser` is unavailable to workers, so the parse would need a different XML stack for a win limited to very large files; the parse yields per part and stays on the main thread.

## Consequences

Real documents flow like Word: paragraphs break mid-sentence at line edges with widow and orphan lines protected, tables continue across pages under a repeated header row, styled tables draw their banded look, headings hold their followers, and footers count from the section's own start. Line density tracks the actual typeface: the short fixtures break pages exactly where Word does, and the five-page fixture drifts one page over its length because Word's document-grid pitch adds per-line space the projection does not model. The known-limitations lists in both READMEs name what remains: mid-row table splits, anchored-drawing offsets and text wrapping, cached field results beyond `PAGE`/`NUMPAGES`, equations, OLE objects, text boxes, footnotes, custom tab stops, embedded fonts, document grids, and full bidi mirroring. Coverage pins every new behaviour: paragraph and table division with the widow rule and header repeat, grid-column merge matching with orphan continuation, band resolution under `tblLook`, story inheritance and the blank first-page header, continuous merging, `pgNumType` numbering, the link scheme whitelist, split-hook pagination including the keepNext-precedence and degenerate-height cases, and the flow-margin arithmetic.
